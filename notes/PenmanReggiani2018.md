## Layer 1 Source Note

Source claim: Penman and Reggiani interpret value and growth labels through accounting fundamentals, arguing that valuation ratios embed both expected earnings growth and the risk that this growth will not be realized (PenmanReggiani2018, p. 103).
Source claim: The paper argues that a high book-to-price ratio can signal risky growth conditional on earnings-to-price, which helps explain why apparently cheap value stocks may become value traps (PenmanReggiani2018, p. 103).
Source claim: Their mechanism depends on how accounting recognition and conservatism shape book value and earnings, so financial statement variables are treated as economically structured signals rather than arbitrary predictors (PenmanReggiani2018, pp. 103--104).
Commentary: This source is useful for motivating why fundamental ratios may contain crash or implosion information beyond generic technical price signals.
Caution: The paper explains value-trap risk rather than directly modeling catastrophic stock implosions, so its role is conceptual support for feature interpretation.
