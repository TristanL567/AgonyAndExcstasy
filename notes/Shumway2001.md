## Layer 1 Source Note

Source claim: Shumway argues that single-period bankruptcy models are inappropriate for multiperiod bankruptcy data because firms' characteristics evolve over time (Shumway2001, p. 101).
Source claim: The paper proposes a discrete-time hazard model and argues that it produces more consistent bankruptcy probability estimates than static classification models (Shumway2001, p. 101 and Section II).
Source claim: Shumway finds that market variables such as size, prior stock returns, and idiosyncratic return variability add predictive power alongside accounting ratios (Shumway2001, p. 101).
Commentary: This source is a bridge between classic accounting-ratio bankruptcy prediction and the thesis's modern event-prediction pipeline, especially because the thesis also uses time-indexed firm-year data.
Caution: Its target is legal bankruptcy, so the thesis must explain why catastrophic implosion or CSI labels are related but not identical outcomes.
