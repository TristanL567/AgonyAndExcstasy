## Layer 1 Source Note

Source claim: Cembalest studies concentrated single-stock ownership and argues that large permanent impairments are common enough to make diversification a central risk-management issue for individual equity holders (Cembalest2014, Executive Summary, p. 1).
Source claim: The report defines catastrophic loss around severe peak-to-trough decline with limited recovery, then applies that framing to Russell 3000 constituents and sector case studies (Cembalest2014, pp. 1, 4, 10--31).
Source claim: The report emphasizes that many eventual losers were plausible operating businesses before their reversals, which makes hindsight narratives unreliable for ex ante risk control (Cembalest2014, p. 1 and sector case studies).
Commentary: This source motivates the thesis focus on catastrophic stock outcomes as a portfolio-management problem rather than only as a corporate bankruptcy or credit-risk problem.
Caution: It is practitioner research rather than peer-reviewed academic evidence, so it should be used for motivation and empirical context, not as the sole methodological authority.
