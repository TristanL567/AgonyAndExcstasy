## Layer 1 Source Note

Source claim: Campbell, Hilscher, and Szilagyi estimate firm failure probabilities with a dynamic logit model using both accounting and market variables, then test how high-distress stocks perform in the cross-section (CampbellHilscherSzilagyi2008, abstract; pp. 2899--2901).
Source claim: Their failure definition is broader than legal bankruptcy because it also includes financially driven delistings and default ratings, which makes the outcome more relevant to equity investors than court bankruptcy alone (CampbellHilscherSzilagyi2008, pp. 2900--2901).
Source claim: They report that high-distress stocks have low realized returns despite high volatility and factor loadings, which challenges the interpretation of distress as a compensated risk premium (CampbellHilscherSzilagyi2008, abstract; pp. 2899--2900).
Commentary: This paper is directly relevant to the thesis because it links distress prediction, equity-market outcomes, and the investor-facing consequences of distress labels.
Caution: The paper studies distress-return pricing, not an explicit exclusion index, so its results motivate the problem but do not by themselves validate a threshold-based index construction rule.
